## Executive Summary

**Top 3 Recommendations (Ranked by Risk-Adjusted ROI):**

1. **Puglia/Sicily, Italy** - Best balance of yield (7-10% gross), mature tourism market, EU/Eurozone stability, and manageable regulatory environment. Properties available from EUR 80-180k. The cedolare secca flat tax (21%) simplifies taxation. Climate risk is moderate but manageable.

2. **Algarve, Portugal (Lagos/Albufeira)** - Strong brand recognition, 5.6-8% gross yields, well-established property management infrastructure, direct flights from Amsterdam. Properties tight at EUR 200-250k budget. Regulatory landscape recently stabilized after 2024 reforms.

3. **Krakow/Gdansk, Poland** - Highest pure rental yields (8-9% gross Airbnb), lowest entry prices (EUR 80-150k), strong appreciation potential, EU/near-Eurozone, short flights from Amsterdam. Risk: currency exposure (PLN), seasonal demand, upcoming EU STR regulation.

**Key Findings:**
- Net yields after all costs (management, taxes, maintenance, platform fees) typically land at 3-6%, roughly half of gross yields
- The EU's new STR Regulation 2024/1028 (effective May 2026) fundamentally changes the compliance landscape across all EU countries
- Mediterranean climate risk is real but primarily affects summer peak season comfort, not property viability in the 10-year horizon
- Dutch Box 3 taxation (effective ~2.16% on property value) applies to all foreign property regardless of local taxes paid, though double taxation relief is available
- Property management costs of 15-25% of revenue are the single largest drag on returns after taxes

### Detailed Location Profiles

#### A. Puglia, Italy
- **What EUR 250k buys:** A renovated 2-3 bedroom trullo or apartment in/near Ostuni, Lecce, or Polignano a Mare. Or purchase at EUR 100-150k + EUR 25-40k renovation.
- **Tourist appeal:** Gastronomy, beaches, UNESCO heritage, increasingly popular with Northern Europeans
- **Peak season:** June-September, shoulder season April-May and October
- **Off-season:** Very quiet Nov-Mar, minimal rental income
- **Key advantage:** Low entry price allows significant budget headroom; Bari airport well-connected

#### B. Sicily, Italy
- **What EUR 250k buys:** A spacious apartment or small house in Catania, Palermo, Syracuse, or Cefalù
- **Tourist appeal:** History, volcanoes, beaches, food culture, Game of Thrones tourism
- **Key advantage:** Catania has major airport, rising tourism, lowest prices among popular Italian destinations
- **Risk:** Some areas have infrastructure challenges; Mafia perception (declining but present)

#### C. Lagos/Albufeira, Algarve, Portugal
- **What EUR 250k buys:** A 1-2 bedroom apartment (tight at current prices of EUR 3,467/m2)
- **Tourist appeal:** World-class beaches, golf, mild winters, established expat community
- **Peak revenue:** July-August (EUR 6,000+/month), low season dips to EUR 1,400/month
- **Key advantage:** Longest season in Southern Europe; digital nomad appeal extends winter bookings
- **Risk:** Prices have risen significantly; budget is at the floor for decent properties

#### D. Krakow, Poland
- **What EUR 250k buys:** A premium 2-3BR apartment in the Old Town or Kazimierz district
- **Tourist appeal:** UNESCO Old Town, Jewish Quarter, vibrant nightlife, cheap destination for Western Europeans
- **Airbnb revenue:** ~PLN 4,900/month (~EUR 1,140), 75% peak occupancy
- **Key advantage:** Year-round city tourism (culture, stag parties, conferences); EUR 250k buys a top-tier property
- **Risk:** Currency risk (PLN); regulatory uncertainty; competition from new supply

#### E. Gdansk, Poland
- **What EUR 250k buys:** Excellent 2-3BR apartment in historic center or waterfront
- **Tourist appeal:** Baltic beaches, historic architecture, amber, rising summer destination
- **Key advantage:** More seasonal but less competition than Krakow; strong summer yields
- **Risk:** Winter occupancy much lower; currency risk

#### F. Split, Croatia
- **What EUR 250k buys:** A 1-2BR apartment in or near the old town (prices are elevated)
- **Tourist appeal:** Diocletian's Palace, islands, Game of Thrones, sailing
- **Airbnb revenue:** Median EUR 32k/year, 75% occupancy
- **Key advantage:** Croatia joined Eurozone (2023); strong tourism brand; excellent connectivity
- **Risk:** New property tax (EUR 0.60-8.00/m2); operating costs eat 35-50% of gross; seasonal

#### G. Riga, Latvia
- **What EUR 250k buys:** Premium 3+ bedroom apartment in Art Nouveau district or Old Town
- **Tourist appeal:** Architecture, affordability, Baltic culture, stag/hen parties
- **Key advantage:** Highest long-term rental yields in EU (8.5%+); Eurozone member; very cheap entry
- **Risk:** Harsh winters limit tourist season; market depth is limited; geopolitical proximity to Russia

#### H. Sofia, Bulgaria
- **What EUR 250k buys:** Luxury apartment or multiple investment units
- **Tourist appeal:** Growing tech hub, digital nomads, Vitosha mountain, cheap city break
- **Key advantage:** Lowest property tax in EU (~0.15%); Schengen entry 2024 boosted connectivity
- **Risk:** Not Eurozone (BGN pegged to EUR); limited tourist brand; lower capital appreciation

#### I. Budva/Kotor, Montenegro
- **What EUR 250k buys:** A 1-2BR apartment with sea views in Budva or Bay of Kotor
- **Tourist appeal:** Stunning coastline, old towns, emerging luxury destination
- **Key advantage:** No restrictions on apartment purchases; 15% flat tax; strong appreciation (23% YoY)
- **Risk:** Not EU; small market; very seasonal (Jun-Sep); limited mortgage availability

#### J. Vlora/Saranda, Albania
- **What EUR 250k buys:** Multiple apartments or a premium sea-view property
- **Tourist appeal:** Albanian Riviera, untouched beaches, extremely affordable
- **Key advantage:** Highest gross yields (8-11%); new Vlora airport (2025); lowest entry costs
- **Risk:** NOT EU; cadastral/legal risks; corruption; infrastructure gaps; property title disputes

### Dutch Box 3 Impact

**Critical for the buyer:** All foreign property is declared in Dutch Box 3.
- **Deemed return on property:** 6.00% of property value (2025)
- **Tax rate:** 36%
- **Effective tax:** ~2.16% of property value annually
- **Tax-free threshold:** EUR 57,684 (2025), EUR 51,396 (2026)
- **Double taxation relief:** Available via treaty — the foreign property portion of Box 3 liability is proportionally exempted
- **Net effect:** If the local country's effective tax rate exceeds the Dutch deemed rate, no additional Dutch tax is due. For most countries listed, local rental income tax + property tax exceeds the Box 3 deemed amount, so the effective additional Dutch burden is minimal to zero.

### Dutch Mortgage for Foreign Property

A Dutch-based mortgage for a foreign property is generally **not available** through standard Dutch lenders. Options:
- **Local mortgage in target country** (see above)
- **Equity release** on existing Dutch property (hypotheekverhoging) — if buyer has a Dutch home with equity
- **Cash purchase** — simplest and most common for this budget range
- **International mortgage brokers** like Traverse International Finance specialize in cross-border mortgages

### Key Climate Projections

- **Mediterranean summer tourism** may decline 10% by 2050 as summers become "too hot" for comfort
- **Tourists shifting northward** — Northern/Central Europe gaining share
- **Wildfire risk:** Portugal and Spain face the highest wildfire risk in Europe; Algarve is particularly exposed
- **Water scarcity:** Southern Mediterranean faces potential 28-47% water deficit by 2030
- **Insurance costs:** Rising across Southern Europe, though specific data for tourist properties is limited
- **Positive for Northern locations:** Poland, Latvia, and Bulgaria may benefit from climate-driven tourism redistribution

### Impact on Investment Thesis

Mediterranean locations (Italy, Portugal, Spain, Greece, Croatia) face a structural headwind from climate change. However:
- The 10-year investment horizon (2026-2036) is likely manageable — extreme impacts accelerate post-2040
- Shoulder seasons may extend (warmer springs/autumns), partially offsetting summer peak decline
- Northern European alternatives benefit from the shift
- **Net assessment:** Climate risk should **discount** Mediterranean yields by ~1% in long-term projections, and **boost** Polish/Latvian/Bulgarian projections

## 8. Practical Considerations

### Property Management Options

**Full-service management (handles everything: listings, guests, cleaning, maintenance):**
- Typical cost: 20-30% of gross rental income (Southern Europe)
- Lower in Eastern Europe: 15-25%
- Includes: guest communication, check-in/out, cleaning, laundry, minor repairs, listing optimization

**Half-service / co-hosting:**
- Typical cost: 10-15% of gross rental income
- Owner handles bookings/communication; local partner handles cleaning and access

**Self-management (remote from NL):**
- Requires: smart locks, local cleaner (EUR 40-80 per turnover), automated messaging
- Feasible for: experienced hosts with reliable local contacts
- Cost: ~5-10% of revenue (cleaning + supplies + tools)
- Risk: guest emergencies, maintenance issues require trusted local contact

## 11. Recommended Next Steps

### Immediate Actions (Month 1-2)
1. **Decide on 2-3 target locations** based on personal risk tolerance and capital available
2. **Visit the top locations** — combine with short vacation; view properties, meet agents
3. **Consult a Dutch tax advisor** specializing in international property (e.g., Taxsight, NLCompass) to understand personal Box 3 impact
4. **Budget realistically:** Purchase price + 10-15% closing costs + EUR 8-15k furnishing + 3-month cash reserve

### Due Diligence Phase (Month 2-4)
5. **Engage a local lawyer** in the target country (independent of seller/agent)
6. **Verify STR licensing requirements** in the specific municipality — not just country-level rules
7. **Interview 2-3 property management companies** in the target area; ask for references and actual client performance data
8. **Run a detailed financial model** with the specific property, local tax rates, and management quotes

### Execution Phase (Month 4-8)
9. **Secure financing** if applicable (local mortgage or Dutch equity release)
10. **Complete purchase** with local lawyer oversight
11. **Furnish and photograph** — professional photos are essential for Airbnb success
12. **List on multiple platforms** (Airbnb + Booking.com minimum)
13. **Set up automated pricing** (PriceLabs, Beyond Pricing, or Wheelhouse)

### Ongoing Management
14. **Review performance quarterly** — adjust pricing, respond to reviews, optimize listing
15. **File taxes in both countries** — local income tax + Dutch Box 3 declaration
16. **Annual property inspection** — combine with personal use (1-2 weeks/year)
